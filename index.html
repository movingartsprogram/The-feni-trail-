You have probably had bad feni. Almost everyone has.

Served warm, poured heavy, handed to you as a dare on your second night in Goa. That drink has almost nothing to do with the one this trip is about.

Feni is distilled from cashew apples and coconut sap, has been made here since the 1600s, and carries India's Geographical Indication tag along with the state's own title of Heritage Spirit — the same protection that sits behind Scotch and Champagne. Four centuries of it, and no serious way to taste it properly.

So we built one.

What this is

Not a distillery tour. Not a bar crawl. Nine hours of driving to see eleven things is somebody else's idea of a good trip.

The Feni Trail moves slowly and stops in few places, because the places are the point. You sleep on the plantation where the spirit is made. You watch the still run. You eat fish grilled by a man who has been feeding this coast for decades, and you sit in a home bar where a couple in their seventies have been distilling for fifty years.

In between there is a guitar under open sky, a 350-year-old mansion, and a pool with a DJ at it. That is the whole itinerary. It is meant to be.

The doors this opens are opened by people we know. That is the product — not the route.

Day One

Into the orchards

Early afternoon

We collect you

Private pickup from your hotel or villa in South Goa, then the drive inland. Nobody starts a trip like this in a taxi they arranged themselves.

Afternoon

Dudhsagar Plantation

An organic farmstay in South Goa's forest belt, awarded Gold for Sustainable Leadership at the Outlook Traveler Responsible Tourism Awards. Settle in, freshen up, let the drive fall away. It is hills and quiet from here.

Late afternoon

The jungle, the waterfall, the still

A walk through the plantation — waterfalls tucked into the green, cashew and coconut heavy with fruit, and at the centre of it the copper still. This is where Ouro is born, Portuguese for gold, made by the same family for three generations. Fruit crushed, juice fermented, double-distilled over a wood fire. Ask the distiller anything; he would rather you did.

Golden hour

High tea at the farmhouse

Goan snacks, fresh juices, low light, no agenda. This is the hour where twenty strangers stop being twenty strangers.

8 PM onwards

The barbecue, and Elvis Lobo

The sound of Goa, solo and acoustic under open sky — and a barbecue running alongside it, plate after plate, with feni cocktails poured through the evening. No stage, no distance, no seating plan. Twenty people close enough to hear the strings, eating while he plays. Then, full and unhurried, everyone turns in.

Down to the coast

Morning

Breakfast at the farm

Slow mornings only. Breakfast at the farmhouse, then checkout and south towards the sea.

1 PM · Colva

The home bar

One hour, somewhere no guidebook sends you. A couple who have been married, and distilling, for decades — seventy-five years old, fifty of them spent getting this right. The spirit exactly as it is meant to be shared: at somebody's table, not across a counter. The stop most guests talk about afterwards.

2:30 PM until 7

Pele's, on the sand

Four and a half hours at a beachside institution that has fed cricket legends, film stars and politicians without changing a thing about how it grills fish. Eat, swim, sleep in a chair, do nothing at all. This is the longest stop on the Trail and there is no schedule inside it — that is the point of it.

7:30 PM

A guided walk through a 350-year-old mansion that has quietly watched Goa change for three and a half centuries.

8:30 PM until midnight

Jac's Tavern

DJ Voyager takes over by the pool and the night runs to twelve without anyone checking a watch. Then we drive you home.

8 PM, under open sky

Elvis Lobo

The sound of Goa

Some places have a landscape, a cuisine and a spirit. Goa also has a sound — and for a generation of people who grew up here, a good deal of that sound has come off Elvis Lobo's guitar. He is a cultural institution in a state that does not hand that title out lightly, and one of the artists Moving Arts Program has built its concert series around since 2019.

On the Trail he plays a private set from eight. Solo, acoustic, no stage and no PA to hide behind. The barbecue runs alongside it and the feni cocktails keep coming, so nobody sits in rows and nobody waits for dinner — you eat, you drink, and he plays, all in the same hour, on a plantation with nothing else around it. This is the emotional centre of the two days, and the one thing on the itinerary that genuinely cannot be booked by anyone else.

Everything on Day One is warm-up for eight o'clock.

Before you ask

Questions

I don't actually like feni.

Most people who say that have only had bad feni, which is most of the feni sold in Goa. Fixing that is half the point. And if we fail — the plantation, the music, Pele's and a 350-year-old mansion stand up entirely on their own.

Is that really everything?

Yes. ₹20,000 a head, or ₹30,000 for two travelling together. That covers transfers from your hotel, a night on the plantation, every meal, every drink, a private acoustic set by Elvis Lobo, and transport across all of it. Nothing is added at the end and there is nothing to tip.

There are six of us. Can you run it privately?

Not at this price — the economics need the group. Either join a scheduled running with others, or take all twenty seats and the whole Trail is yours. Companies and celebration groups usually do the second.

How much drinking is this, honestly?

Tasting pours, not volume. There is food at every stop that serves alcohol, water throughout, and a properly designed alternative if you don't drink — kokum, tender coconut, fresh juice, not an apology and a lime soda. Guests who arrive at the home bar sober enjoy it considerably more.

What do I need to be able to do?

Walk comfortably on uneven ground for about an hour — plantation paths, a waterfall, a beach, and an old mansion with old staircases. Tell us at booking if that's difficult and we'll work around it. Tell us about allergies and anything you don't eat, too: there is a barbecue on Day One and an afternoon of seafood on Day Two, and we'd rather know a fortnight early than at the grill.

When does it run?

October to May, outside the monsoon, roughly once every two months. Twenty seats each time. Dates go to the enquiry list before they go anywhere else.
